Restaurant policies and terms are generally divided into customer-facing terms of service and internal employee handbooks that ensure legal compliance and operational consistency.


Customer Terms and Conditions
Online Ordering: Restaurants typically disclaim warranties for online orders, stating services are provided "as is," and reserve the right to refuse orders or modify prices without notice.
Liability: Terms often exclude liability for indirect damages, data breaches, or delays, and require customers to keep their account details confidential.
Reservations: Policies may require deposits for large parties, specify grace periods for arrivals, and outline cancellation fees or forfeiture of deposits for no-shows.
Conduct: Restaurants reserve the right to refuse service or remove patrons who are abusive, violate dress codes, or fail to inform staff of allergies.

Employee Policies and Compliance

Labor Laws: Employers must adhere to the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) regarding minimum wage, overtime, and tip pooling rules, which vary significantly by state (e.g., some states prohibit tip credits).
Safety and Health: Staff must follow OSHA guidelines for workplace safety and FDA Food Code standards for hygiene, including handwashing, temperature control, and allergen awareness.
Handbook Essentials: Standard employee handbooks include codes of conduct, dress codes, scheduling and attendance rules, disciplinary procedures, and clear job descriptions.
Training: Many jurisdictions mandate food handler certifications and regular anti-harassment or safety training, with HR required to maintain verifiable compliance records.
